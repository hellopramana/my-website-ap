## Back in 2018
I was young and so ambisious with my visual taste. I believed what I think good, is good.  After graduating from high school, I got my first job as photo editor. I was so excited. This is a small team—only 2 photographers and 2 editors. But things will not always go as you expect. In those times, I got frustated badly, following a style that was not my taste at all.
<br>

## What teams needed vs what you want
While I referenced so much on travel photography, my job is to edit baby photoshoots, maternity, and family photos that were shot inside a photo studio. Both things are about composing light—natural lighting vs artificial lighting. But that never cross my mind until many problems that I made. Sometimes, the skintone is too yellow because I tried to make the photo have a warm tone rather than make it balanced. And there was striped skin. I should have made it even, but I kept it as natural as it is. I believed that skin should like that because that's what it is.        
<br>
My senior editor always checked my work and gave feedback. Of course, sometimes he missed things. And then clients complain. They didn't like when the skin was not smooth. They wanted to look slim in the photos. They asked, "Why the scars and acne not removed?" The photo was too yellow and etc. I believed what I had done was already great in my eyes, but did it already meet the objective of the team? Were my clients happy with the result?      
<br>
Those are some of the reasons why we shouldn't force our idealsm into our work. We should know our project objectives, who we design for, and how we should execute it. To be a [[Grow as a designer|better designer]], we should think about those on top of our mind. Don't let your idealism makes you defensive towards feedback.       
<br>
## Don't delete your idealism.
No matter how good your design taste or your visual art is, if you only think about your tase, you only hold yourself back from growing. Gather as many feedbacks as you can. Great design is design that is helpful for the user. But we don't want to put our idealism away—we want to express it. So if you want to express it, you can make personal projects. Do what you can't do in your main job, be wild, and make a home for your ideas which have not been realized. Don't forget to share it. In this way, your will find your own audience that appreciate your perspective.
