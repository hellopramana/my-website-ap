<br>

Every design project looks different on the surface. Different clients, different industries, different goals. But the way I start? Almost always the same.

<br>

Over time, I’ve learned that how you begin a project often determines how smoothly it runs. So instead of jumping straight into Figma or browsing inspiration, I follow a simple structure that keeps everything aligned from day one.

Here’s how I start every design project.

## 1. I Clarify the Real Goal (Not Just the Request)

Clients usually come with a request:  
“I need a website.”  
“I want a logo.”  
“I need a landing page.”

But what they actually need is rarely just that.

So the first thing I do is ask:

- What problem are we solving?
- Who is the target audience?
- What action do we want users to take?
- How will we measure success?

Design is not decoration. It’s a tool. And tools only work well when you know what they’re built for.

## 2. I Understand the Audience Before Touching the Layout

Before thinking about colors or typography, I think about people.

Who are they?  
What are they struggling with?  
What do they expect?  
What would make them trust this brand?

If I design based on my own taste, the project becomes subjective.  
If I design based on user behavior, the project becomes strategic.

That shift changes everything.

## 3. I Gather Context and Constraints

Good design doesn’t happen in a vacuum.

I look at:

- Existing brand guidelines
- Competitors
- Technical limitations
- Timeline
- Budget
- Content availability

Constraints aren’t creative blockers. They’re boundaries that help focus the solution.

The clearer the constraints, the stronger the outcome.

## 4. I Define Structure Before Style

This is where many designers rush.

Before choosing fonts or building visuals, I map out:

- Information hierarchy
- User flow
- Wireframes
- Content structure

If the structure is weak, no visual polish will save it.

I want the design to work in black and white before it works in color.

## 5. I Align Early, Not Late

One of the biggest lessons I’ve learned: don’t wait until the design is “perfect” to show it.

I share early concepts.  
I validate direction.  
I ask for feedback on structure before styling.

It saves time. It prevents major revisions. And it builds trust.

---

## My Philosophy

Starting well is more important than finishing fast.

When the foundation is strong, everything else becomes smoother:

- Fewer revisions
- Clearer communication
- Better user experience
- Happier clients

Design is not just about making things look good. It’s about thinking clearly before making anything at all.

And that’s how I start every design project.
